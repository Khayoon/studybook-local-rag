# Interpreting practice with a study partner

This original demonstration guide describes informal language exercises. It makes no claim about court procedures, certification requirements, or professional qualifications.

## Practice a short consecutive turn

Ask a partner to read two or three sentences about an everyday topic, such as planning a class or rescheduling a meeting. Listen to the full turn, then convey the meaning in the other language. Your partner can compare the main message, names, numbers, and relationships between ideas.

For this exercise, use a 30-second source turn. This is only a demonstration setting. Shorten it when you need to isolate a particular difficulty.

## Practice reading aloud in another language

Choose a short written passage. Read it silently, identify its main idea, and explain it aloud in the other language. Mark unfamiliar expressions for the glossary notebook. Revisit the passage after checking those expressions.

## A review checklist

- Did I preserve the speaker's main point?
- Did I retain the important names, numbers, and contrasts?
- Did I add an explanation that was absent from the original?
- Which phrase would I like to practice again?

The checklist is for reflection; it is not a scoring rubric for a professional test.

## Connect practice to the learning journal

After each attempt, record one concrete next action. For example: "Review the five glossary entries about scheduling, then repeat the meeting passage." Keep the source passage and your notes together so you can compare attempts over time.
